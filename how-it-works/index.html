How to humanize AI text without losing meaning

Humanizing AI text means changing how it is written so that it reads like a person wrote it, without changing what it says. You can do it by hand, try it with a prompt, or use a tool. Here is what each one takes.

What makes text read as AI

Mostly evenness. The sentences are similar in length, the same linking words keep appearing, and every paragraph opens with a summary and closes with a lesson. Nothing is wrong with any single sentence. Together they are too regular to be a person. Signs of AI writing goes through these one by one.

How to humanize AI text by hand

For a short piece this works well. Go through the draft once for each step.

  1. 1Write down your point first. Before you edit a paragraph, say in one rough sentence of your own what it is for. Keep that sentence in.
  2. 2Break the rhythm. Put a four-word sentence next to a thirty-word one.
  3. 3Cut the stock linking words: furthermore, moreover, additionally, in conclusion.
  4. 4Trade a general claim for one concrete detail: a number, a name, a moment.
  5. 5Delete the tidy last sentence of each paragraph and see if anything is lost.
  6. 6Read it aloud. Anything you would never say out loud, rewrite.
  7. 7Check the facts against the original: numbers, names, dates and quotes.

A small example. The AI draft says: "The existing literature on corporate governance has extensively explored mechanisms designed to mitigate agency problems between managers and controlling shareholders." A person would more likely write: "Earlier work on corporate governance has looked closely at the ways firms keep managers and large owners in check." Same claim, plainer words, a different shape.

The catch is time. Doing this properly across a long document is slow, and it is hard to judge your own text.

Can a prompt humanize AI text?

Partly. Asking a chatbot to sound more human, vary its sentence length or write casually does change the tone. In our tests it rarely changed how the text scored, because the same model is still making the same kinds of choices about what to say and in what order.

A prompt works better when you give it something of yours to start from: your own rough sentences, your own order of points, a paragraph you wrote before. Then it is polishing your writing instead of producing its own. We collected ten prompts that work this way.

Shortcuts that do not work

  1. 1Swapping words for synonyms. The sentence shapes stay the same, and so does the result.
  2. 2Translating the text into another language and back. It adds errors and keeps the structure.
  3. 3Planting typos or odd words. A detector might move. A reader will notice.
  4. 4Hidden characters and look-alike letters. They break when the text is copied, and they are easy to catch.

Which method to use

For a paragraph or a short email, do it by hand. It takes ten minutes and you stay in control of every word. If you have rough notes or sentences of your own, a prompt can tidy them without taking over. For a long document, or when you cannot see what is wrong with your own draft, a tool is faster and more consistent.

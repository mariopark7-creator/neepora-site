What an AI bypasser has to do

AI detectors do not match your text against a list of words. They measure how predictable the writing is and how even it is. So to bypass AI detection, the writing itself has to change: sentence length, rhythm, word choice, the way one point leads to the next.

That is why swapping synonyms rarely works. It is also why the shortcuts are a bad deal. Planted typos, odd characters and scrambled phrasing can move a score, but a human reader spots them in a second.

neepora takes the slower route. It rewrites the text the way people write, and leaves your facts, numbers, names and quotes where they were.

An AI detection remover, without removing your meaning

People also look for an AI remover or an AI detection remover. Nothing is removed in the literal sense. A detector flags the pattern of the writing, and the only dependable fix for a pattern is a rewrite.

The risk with a careless rewrite is that it removes the wrong thing: a date, a figure, half of your closing paragraph. Our before-and-after tests show what that looks like.

When to use an AI bypasser, and when not to

Good reasons exist. Your own writing gets flagged, which happens most to formal writers and people writing in a second language. You drafted with AI help for work or for the web, where that is allowed, and you want it to read like you. You are polishing a translation.

A rewrite does not change the rules, though. If a school, employer or publisher bans AI help or asks you to disclose it, that still applies after the text has been humanized. Read our responsible use page.

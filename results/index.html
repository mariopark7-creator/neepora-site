The short answer

Mostly yes, if the only question is the score. Most AI humanizers can move a detector result. The harder question is what happened to the text on the way.

In the examples above, both versions read as human to the detector. Only one of them still has the date, the right population and the closing line of the email.

How we test

The same check each time, and you can repeat it with any tool.

  1. 1Take a paragraph of unedited AI text and mark the facts in it: dates, numbers, names, what is being claimed.
  2. 2Run the same paragraph through each humanizer with default settings.
  3. 3Score every result with an AI detector.
  4. 4Read each result against the original, line by line, and tick off the marked facts.
  5. 5Note anything added, dropped or changed, and anything a reader would trip over.

What goes wrong with AI humanizers

Facts change

"Over a million inhabitants" becomes "millions of poor citizens". The sentence still sounds fine. It is just no longer true.

Content disappears

Whole sentences are cut to make the text less regular. In the email above, the closing was deleted.

Errors are planted

Deliberate typos and odd word choices can lower a score. They also make you look careless.

Hidden characters

Invisible characters and look-alike letters fool some detectors until the next update, and break when the text is copied.

What no AI humanizer can do

It cannot guarantee a result on every detector. Detectors are built differently, change often and disagree with each other, so a promise of 100% is a red flag.

It cannot check your facts for you or decide what you are allowed to submit. Read the result before you use it, and see our responsible use page for where a humanizer fits and where it does not.

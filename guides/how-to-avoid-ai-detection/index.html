How to avoid AI detection in your writing

Eight editing steps that lower AI detection scores, why they work, and the popular shortcuts that do not.

Short answer

To avoid AI detection, make your writing less predictable. Vary sentence length, replace stock phrases and safe vocabulary with the words you would actually say, add specific details from your own experience, and rewrite whole sentences rather than swapping synonyms. Tricks like hidden characters or deliberate typos are unreliable and easy to spot.

Why AI detectors flag text

Detectors look for writing that is statistically easy to predict. Language models choose likely words and produce sentences of similar length and shape, so their output scores low on surprise and low on variation. Human writing is lumpier: a short sentence after a long one, an unexpected word, a detail no one else would include. Avoiding detection means putting that lumpiness back. More detail is in how AI detectors work.

8 steps to avoid AI detection

  1. 1Start from your own outline. Decide your main point and the order of your arguments yourself. Structure copied from a model tends to follow the same predictable shape.
  2. 2Rewrite whole sentences, not words. Change the subject of the sentence, the order of clauses or the verb. Swapping single words for synonyms leaves the underlying pattern intact.
  3. 3Vary sentence length on purpose. In each paragraph, aim for at least one sentence under 10 words and one over 25. Read it aloud; if every sentence takes the same breath, change one.
  4. 4Cut stock transitions. Delete "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion" and "It is important to note". Most paragraphs read better without them.
  5. 5Replace general claims with specifics. Swap "many people" for a number, "a recent study" for the actual study, "in many industries" for one named example.
  6. 6Use your natural voice. Contractions, the first person where it fits, and the plain word you would use in conversation all raise unpredictability.
  7. 7Reorder ideas. Move your strongest point to the start, or open with an example instead of a definition. Models usually build in the same predictable order.
  8. 8Check, then fix only what is flagged. Run the text through a detector that highlights sentences, rewrite the flagged ones, and re-read the whole piece to make sure the meaning did not change.

What does not work

These shortcuts are widely shared online. Most of them either fail against current detectors or make the text worse in ways a human reader notices immediately.

ShortcutWhy it fails
Synonym swappingThe sentence structure and rhythm stay the same, and odd synonyms make the text harder to read.
Hidden characters or look-alike lettersSome tools strip them before scoring, and they break copy and paste, search and spell check.
Deliberate typosThey lower quality for every reader and do little to the overall pattern a detector measures.
Translating to another language and backMeaning drifts and the result is often still smooth and predictable.
Asking a model to "write like a human"The output usually follows the model's own defaults again: even sentences, the same stock phrases.

Independent testing found that detector accuracy drops once text is paraphrased or edited by hand, which is why real editing is the approach that holds up.

When you should disclose AI use instead

If your school, employer or publisher bans AI assistance or requires you to disclose it, follow that rule. No editing method changes what the rule asks of you, and getting around it is a breach of those rules and of most tools' terms of use, including ours. These steps are for writers whose own work is being flagged, and for drafts where AI help is allowed.

Frequently asked questions

Is it possible to make AI text completely undetectable?

No method guarantees it. Detectors differ from each other and change over time. Heavily edited text written in your own voice scores lowest, but any score is a probability, not a certainty.

Does paraphrasing avoid AI detection?

Paraphrasing lowers detection rates in independent tests, but simple synonym swapping does much less than rewriting sentence structure. Paraphrased text can still be flagged.

What makes writing sound less like AI?

Varied sentence length, plain everyday words, specific details, a clear personal voice and fewer stock transitions such as Moreover and In conclusion.

Do AI detectors look for hidden characters?

Most detectors score the statistical pattern of the words, not hidden characters. Some tools remove invisible characters before scoring, so adding or removing them rarely changes the result.

How do I avoid AI detection without using AI?

Write from your own outline, vary your sentence length, cut filler phrases and add specific details. Keep your drafts and version history in case your work is questioned.

Can a teacher tell if I used AI even if a detector does not?

Often, yes. A sudden change in style, vocabulary or quality compared with your earlier work, or not being able to explain your own argument, is noticeable to a reader regardless of any detector score.

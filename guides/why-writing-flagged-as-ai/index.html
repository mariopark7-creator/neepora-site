Why is my writing flagged as AI?

The nine most common reasons a detector mistakes human writing for AI, how to spot each one in your own text, and what to do if it already happened.

Short answer

AI detectors do not know who wrote a text. They measure how predictable it is. Writing that uses safe, common word choices, sentences of similar length and stock phrases looks statistically like AI output, so careful, formal or second-language writing is often flagged even when a person wrote every word.

9 reasons human writing gets flagged as AI

Most detectors score a text on two things: how surprising each word is given the words before it, and how much that surprise varies from sentence to sentence. Low surprise and little variation read as machine-written. Every reason below comes back to one of those two measurements.

  1. 1Predictable word choices. When you always pick the most common word for the job ("important", "significant", "various"), each word is easy to guess. Detectors call this low perplexity.
  2. 2Sentences of the same length and shape. Human writing usually mixes long and short sentences. A paragraph of five 20-word sentences built the same way has low burstiness, which is a strong AI signal.
  3. 3Stock transitions and filler. "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In conclusion", "It is important to note that" and "plays a crucial role" appear far more often in AI text than in most human writing.
  4. 4General statements with no specifics. Lines like "technology has changed many aspects of our lives" could appear in any essay. Names, numbers, places and examples from your own work are much harder for a model to predict.
  5. 5Grammar and rewriting tools. Features that rephrase sentences for you smooth out exactly the quirks that make writing look human. A draft you wrote yourself can score as AI after an automatic polish.
  6. 6Formal or template genres. Abstracts, lab reports, cover letters and legal or technical writing follow fixed patterns. Human authors in these genres are also even and predictable.
  7. 7Writing in a second language. People writing in a language they learned later tend to use a smaller vocabulary and simpler structures. In one study, detectors flagged about 61% of essays by non-native English writers as AI on average.
  8. 8Short texts. A paragraph or two gives a detector very little to measure, so its guess swings more. The same writing can score very differently at 100 words and at 600.
  9. 9Mixed drafts. If parts of a text came from an AI tool, even lightly edited, they can pull the score of the whole document up and make your own sentences look suspect by association.

Quick check: patterns and fixes

Read a flagged paragraph against this table. Most flagged passages show two or three of these at once.

PatternWhat it looks likeWhat to change
Even rhythmEvery sentence 15 to 25 words, same structureBreak one long sentence in two; join two short ones; start one with the verb
Stock transitionsMoreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In conclusionDelete them, or say how the ideas connect in plain words
Safe vocabularysignificant, various, crucial, enhance, utilizeUse the word you would say out loud
Generic claims"This has many benefits for society"Name one benefit, one person or one number
Lists of three"clear, concise, and engaging" in every paragraphKeep the one item that matters most
Hedged conclusions"Overall, it is clear that..."End on your strongest specific point instead

What to do if your work was flagged

A detector score is a probability, not proof. Independent testing found that no widely used tool was reliably accurate, and that results got worse on edited text. You are allowed to push back, and the strongest way to do it is with evidence of how you wrote.

  1. 1Collect your drafts. Version history in your document editor, earlier files, notes, outlines and browser history all show a piece being written over time.
  2. 2Ask which sentences were flagged. A single overall percentage is hard to argue with. Specific sentences can be explained.
  3. 3Explain your process in person. Walk through how you researched and wrote it. Being able to discuss your own argument is convincing evidence.
  4. 4Ask for a second method. Point out that detectors disagree with each other and are less reliable on short, formal or second-language writing, and ask that the decision not rest on one score.

For more on how far a score can be trusted, see are AI detectors accurate?.

How to lower the score honestly

You do not need tricks. Edit the way a good editor would: read the flagged sentences aloud, change the rhythm, cut the filler, and add a detail only you would know. Then check the paragraph again with an AI detector that shows sentence-level results so you can see which lines still read as predictable.

Before and after, as an example of the kind of change that helps:

Sentence
BeforeFurthermore, social media plays a crucial role in shaping the opinions of young people in various ways.
AfterMy younger brother changed his mind about a city council vote after one video. That is how fast it happens.

Frequently asked questions

Can an AI detector prove that I used AI?

No. A detector estimates how likely a text is to be machine-written based on statistical patterns. It cannot see how the text was produced, and every detector makes mistakes on human writing.

Why does my writing sound like AI?

Usually because it is very even: similar sentence lengths, common word choices, stock transitions and general statements. Those are the same patterns language models produce by default.

Can grammar tools make my writing get flagged as AI?

They can. Tools that rewrite or rephrase sentences for you remove irregularities that make writing look human. Simple spelling and punctuation fixes have much less effect than full-sentence rewrites.

Do em dashes make writing look like AI?

Some readers treat heavy use of em dashes as a sign of AI writing, but detectors score the whole pattern of word choices, not a single punctuation mark. Using them now and then is not a reason a text gets flagged.

Are non-native English speakers flagged more often?

Yes. A 2023 study found that detectors flagged about 61% of essays by non-native English writers as AI on average, while essays by native speakers were rarely flagged.

How long does a text need to be for a reliable score?

Longer is more reliable. A few sentences give a detector too little to measure; several hundred words give it much more, though no length makes a score certain.

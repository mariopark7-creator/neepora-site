Falsely accused of using AI? What to do

A calm, practical plan for students and writers whose own work was flagged as AI: the evidence that helps, a message you can adapt, and the mistakes to avoid.

Short answer

If you are falsely accused of using AI, do not delete anything or admit to something you did not do. Ask exactly what the accusation is based on, gather evidence of how you wrote the work (version history, drafts, notes, sources), offer to explain your work in person, and point out that AI detectors have documented error rates. If the outcome is still unfair, use the formal appeal process.

8 steps to take if you are falsely accused of using AI

  1. 1Pause and keep everything. Do not delete files, edit the document or clear your browser history. Those records are your evidence.
  2. 2Read the policy. Find your school's or employer's academic integrity or AI policy. Note what counts as a violation, what the process is, your deadlines and your right to respond.
  3. 3Ask for specifics, in writing. Which tool was used, what score it gave, which passages were flagged, and whether anything other than a detector score is part of the case.
  4. 4Collect evidence of your process. Version history, earlier drafts, notes, outlines, sources and timestamps. The table below lists where to find each.
  5. 5Prepare to explain your work. Be ready to summarize your argument, why you chose your sources and how the piece developed. Being able to discuss your own work in detail is strong evidence.
  6. 6Bring the research on detector errors. Independent testing found detection tools were neither accurate nor reliable, and a 2023 study found detectors flagged about 61% of essays by non-native English writers as AI on average.
  7. 7Offer an alternative check. Suggest a conversation about the work, a short writing sample under supervision or a review of your drafts, instead of relying on one score.
  8. 8Appeal formally if needed. If the decision is still against you, follow the written appeal process before the deadline and keep copies of every message.

Evidence that proves your writing process

No single item settles it. Several pieces that show the work growing over time are far more convincing than any detector score.

What to write back

Keep it short, factual and polite. Adapt this to your situation:

Dear [name], Thank you for letting me know about the concern with my [assignment]. I wrote this work myself and I would like the chance to show that. Could you tell me which tool was used, the score it gave and which passages were flagged? I can share my version history, drafts and notes, and I am happy to meet to talk through how I researched and wrote it. I would also ask that the decision not rest on a detector score alone, since independent studies have found these tools make mistakes, especially with formal writing and with writers working in a second language. Kind regards, [your name]

What not to do

  1. 1Do not admit to something you did not do to make the problem go away. It can stay on your record.
  2. 2Do not rewrite or "fix" the submitted work after the accusation, and do not run it through any rewriting tool. Changing it now looks like covering something up.
  3. 3Do not argue only with feelings. Saying you are upset is understandable, but evidence of your process is what changes decisions.
  4. 4Do not miss deadlines. Most appeal processes have short time limits.

How to protect yourself next time

Write in an editor that keeps version history, save your notes and sources, and keep earlier drafts instead of overwriting them. If you want to know in advance how your writing reads to a detector, check it with an AI detector that highlights sentences and revise the flagged parts in your own words. Frequently asked questions

Can a school fail me based only on an AI detector?

It depends on the institution's policy. Detection tools generally describe their scores as indicators rather than proof, and a fair process should look at other evidence, such as drafts and a conversation about the work.

How do I prove I did not use AI?

Show your writing process: version history, earlier drafts, notes, outlines and your research trail, and be ready to explain your work in detail. Several pieces of evidence together are much stronger than any one.

What if I used a grammar or spelling tool?

Say so openly. Spelling and punctuation fixes are usually allowed, but tools that rewrite whole sentences can change how a detector scores your text. Check what your policy says about them.

Should I run my work through other AI detectors?

Results from other detectors can show that tools disagree, but they are not proof either. Evidence of how you wrote the work matters more.

Can non-native English speakers be flagged unfairly?

Yes. A 2023 study found that detectors flagged about 61% of essays by non-native English writers as AI on average, while essays by native speakers were rarely flagged.

Who can help me with an appeal?

Many schools have a student advocate, ombudsperson or student union adviser who can explain the process and attend meetings with you. At work, HR or a union representative can play the same role.

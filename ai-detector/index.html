How to read your AI detection result

The large number is the AI percentage for the whole text: how likely it is that the text as a whole was generated by AI. Under 35% reads as human, 35 to 70% as mixed, over 70% as AI.

The bar below splits your sentences into human, mixed and AI. A text can score as AI overall while still having a few human sentences, which is common when someone edits an AI draft.

In the text itself, each sentence is shaded by how likely it is to be AI. The darker the shade, the more it reads as generated. Hover or tap a sentence to see its number.

What an AI detector looks at

AI detectors do not look for particular words. They measure how predictable the writing is and how even it is: whether every sentence is about the same length, built the same way and uses the expected next word.

People write unevenly. They use a short sentence, then a long one, pick a slightly unexpected word, leave a thought unfinished. That unevenness is what reads as human.

How accurate are AI detectors?

No AI detector is right every time, and ours is no exception. Very short texts, heavily edited texts, translations and very formal writing are the hardest cases. Treat the result as strong evidence, not proof.

If your own writing is flagged, look at the highlighted sentences. They usually share the same pattern: even length and stock phrasing. Rewriting those few sentences often changes the result.

For what independent studies have found, read are AI detectors accurate?

AI detection is not a plagiarism check

A plagiarism checker compares your text with existing sources to find copied passages. An AI detector does not compare anything. It reads the writing itself and estimates whether it was generated. A text can be completely original and still read as AI.
